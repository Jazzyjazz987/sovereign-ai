# Design review — Round 03 (2026-09-05)

23 proposals · 21 survived · 667777 tokens · 13 agents

Facets: model-supply-chain-vetting, production-answer-quality-loop, acceptable-use-safety-incident, measured-capacity-harness, pwa-client-and-notification, tco-budget-governance

## Highest-leverage change

The metadata-only capacity request journal (item 1): a JSONL line per query with token counts, tier, cold/warm, queue depth and phase wall-times, written by an async fire-and-forget task, carrying zero query text and no client identifier, with a ~50-line weekly pandas fit. It is roughly 1.5 days of work, has no RGPD or sovereignty downside (done right it strengthens the CNIL story with verifiable volume and lane-split figures), depends on nothing that is unbuilt, and is the only way to obtain the arrival-rate and burstiness data that every capacity, staffing, SLA and interim-VM decision in the project currently guesses at. It also produces the empirical baseline the production-quality drift detection and the SLA-publication decision are both waiting on. Nothing else in round 3 combines this little cost and risk with this much unblocking."

## Top proposals (ranked, post-critique)

### 1. Metadata-only capacity request journal + weekly arrival-rate fit
_facet: measured-capacity-harness_

**Why:** Arrival rate and its burstiness are the single input nobody in the project has, and every downstream decision that rounds 1-2 deferred (worker-pool size, sync_budget, the 'heures ouvrées best effort' SLA, whether the interim VM is even needed, how the CPU->3090 transition is sized) is currently pure guesswork. Capturing it is near-zero effort, has no PII (scalars, token counts, timestamps, zero query text, no client identifier), depends on nothing unbuilt, and actively strengthens the CNIL story by producing verifiable query-volume and lane-split figures. Its assessment already calls it the strongest of its facet and the one to do first; nothing else in round 3 has this value-to-cost ratio.

**Sketch:** New api/obs/journal.py: an async bounded queue drained by a background task that appends one JSONL line per /query, wired into the existing metrics wrapper in main.py (query_cascade, ~lines 350-363, already the choke point). Fields: ts, tier requested + tier served, prefill_chars, output_chars, model, cold/warm, queue_depth_on_arrival, per-phase wall-times, one thermal reading. Writer is fire-and-forget and must never block or fail a request. Size/age reaper reusing the R2.3 manifest+reaper machinery; add a data_manifest.yaml entry (short TTL, no content, no client id). ~50-line weekly pandas job in api/bench/fit_arrival.py that fits hourly arrival rate, peak-to-mean and the decode-rate distribution and writes an 'observed' block into config/capacity_profile.yaml; cron plus a 'job has not run' alert. ~1.5 days.

### 2. Remove the uncensored community weights now; add config/models.yaml with scoped fail-closed admission control
_facet: model-supply-chain-vetting_

**Why:** dolphin-mixtral is an 'uncensored' fine-tune whose refusal and safety training was deliberately removed, and it is currently the legal/administratif tier answering RGPD and HR questions for 5500 agents with no system prompt at all - the single largest legal and reputational exposure in the stack. It is also Mixtral 8x7B (~26 GB), which does not fit the 22.6 GB budget and is unusable at ~3 tok/s, so removing it costs almost nothing operationally. Separately, config/models.yaml permanently converts the class of bug the phantom 'Llama 3.3 8B' represents - documented-vs-deployed divergence discovered months late - into a one-line diff and a startup assertion, and finally gives the security-critical GLiNER model a named owner.

**Sketch:** Now (~1 day): delete dolphin-mixtral and neural-chat from scripts/ollama_init.sh and the TIERS list in main.py:122-128. LiteLLM already disables T3/T4, so the local cascade caps at T2 until the GPU (consistent with the R1.4/R2 T4-cap intent); if a stopgap T3/T4 is wanted, Jazzy picks the best CPU-viable official small model. Then (~1 day): config/models.yaml, one entry per resident weight (T1, T2, GLiNER, later T3/T4/embedder) with logical tier, upstream ref, immutable content digest (Ollama /api/show sha256, HF commit SHA, GLiNER config hash), quantisation, license, provenance class, host profile (cpu-only | gpu-3090), named owner, French-eval score (nullable until R1.1). Startup checks in main.py and anone_api.py: fail-closed only on GLiNER or T4 digest mismatch; T1-T3 mismatch logs loudly, bumps a counter, fires the T5-moderation webhook, and still serves behind a degradation banner. Emit a divergence report; never auto-write the CLAUDE.md cascade table. Override env flag carries a mandatory dated expiry.

### 3. Minimal cloud-egress substrate: deterministic outbound PII re-scan (fail-closed) + append-only transfer journal
_facet: acceptable-use-safety-incident_

**Why:** Today there is no record of what text crossed to Anthropic, so any Article 33/34 triage must assume the entire corpus leaked, and GLiNER multi_pii-v1 at threshold 0.5 with 6 labels demonstrably misses spaceless NIR, foreign IBAN, +689 numbers and Polynesian surnames. Re-running the R1.2 deterministic structured-PII detector over the outbound anonymised text closes the structured-PII subset of the gap and fails closed to local on any hit; the append-only journal turns 'did subject X's data leave in clear?' into a hash lookup instead of a worst-case assumption. The heavy parts of the original proposal (PWA client-side de-anon, auto-drafted CNIL pack) are deferred; what remains is small and high-value.

**Sketch:** In route_t5_with_anonymization (main.py:254-341), after Anone returns anonymized_query and before the Anthropic call: run the R1.2 regex+checksum detector over anonymized_query; any match -> _fallback_local (fail-closed), increment a leak counter, log-and-alert (not auto-incident yet - tune first). Append one row to a transfer journal (reuse the single Postgres table from item 5): ts, HMAC of the anonymised payload with a retained key, gliner_version, label_set, rescan_result, t5_request_id / approval_id, token_count. Retention 3 years (CNIL limitation), not 'permanent'. Add a test asserting pii_mapping is never logged or returned and stays local to the function. data_manifest.yaml entry for the journal. Blocking prerequisite outside code: Jazzy / the DPO confirm the Chapter V transfer basis (SCCs + TIA, or DPF) before T5 is enabled at all. ~2-3 days.

### 4. PWA as an identity-owned server-side ticket store (corbeille) with a zero-JS progressive-enhancement client
_facet: pwa-client-and-notification_

**Why:** At ~3 tok/s any T2+ answer takes minutes, so a synchronous chat UI is a lie; the ticket model makes latency a first-class fact, matches the R2.6 deferred lane, and needs no websocket/SSE presence layer a 2-3 person team cannot run. The decisive property: if the answer is only ever pulled over the authenticated on-prem channel, the notification never carries content and its transport falls entirely outside the CNIL/sovereignty boundary - it can be plain M365 email. Building the client as server-rendered semantic HTML that works with zero JavaScript makes RGAA 4.1 conformance an architectural property rather than a permanent bug backlog, and runs on the fleet's oldest workstations over satellite. This is the only round-3 proposal that dissolves a hard constraint instead of working around it, which is why it is worth its L-size cost.

**Sketch:** Server: a corbeille table in Postgres keyed to the Entra oid from the R2.1 proxy, ticket states reçue/en-cours/prête/servie, a client-generated UUID as idempotency key against double-submit on flaky links, GET /corbeille?since=cursor with a full-resync path for when TTL has pruned tickets below a stored cursor. Client: semantic HTML forms that fully work with no JS (submit / list / read); the no-JS submit returns 303 straight to the ticket view; JS enhancement is poll + sentence-batched aria-live=polite rendering that never steals focus. 'Cloud autorisé' becomes a request field ('je demande l'usage du cloud pour cette question') that routes to the human T5 gate; the server independently refuses any T5 route for a user without the Entra role regardless of the submitted field. Degradation banner carries a text/icon marker, not colour alone. IndexedDB wiped on logout; draft bodies not persisted on shared-profile deployments. data_manifest.yaml entry classifying the store as potentially special-category with a hard TTL and scripted erasure. New queue-depth and oldest-open-ticket gauges. Optional output language-ID check to suppress incidental Tahitian model output. ~2-3 weeks; gated on the R2.1 proxy landing.

### 5. Consolidated AI TCO line + durable Postgres spend/transfer ledger + two-tier overrun authority
_facet: tco-budget-governance_

**Why:** The T5 ceiling is an in-process counter (_t5_calls) that resets on every restart, so a crash-loop or a second replica has no ceiling and overspend is discovered on the monthly Anthropic invoice; there is also no single named euro owner and no rule for who may lift a cap. The fix is cheap - one table on a database that is already deployed - and the same table doubles as the Article 30 register for the Chapter V transfers that every T5 call legally is. The policy half (named owner, versioned envelope file, alert-authority separated from ceiling-authority) is almost free and piggybacks on the git the team already uses.

**Sketch:** One table t5_ledger on the existing Postgres path (no separate service, no stored procedures): a row per T5 call with the real usage already read at main.py:326 and currently discarded - ts, model, input_tokens, output_tokens, cost_eur, purpose, request_id (idempotent). Post-pay check in route_t5_with_anonymization before the call: SELECT today's and this month's sums, compare to caps stored as rows, over -> _fallback_local with a typed reason; ledger unreachable -> fail-closed to local. Prometheus reads the table for a real spend gauge; alert rules at 70/90/100 percent. Fixed documented USD->EUR rate. Policy in git: config/budget_envelopes.yaml versioned, one named holder (Jazzy / Parc & Assistance), ops receives alerts and may not raise a ceiling, only the holder edits the file and the commit logs who/when/why. ~30-euro plug meter for the electricity line item. Per-identity caps deferred until an auth layer exists. ~2 days.

### 6. Hardware-fingerprinted config/capacity_profile.yaml as the single source of truth for latency constants
_facet: measured-capacity-harness_

**Why:** main.py currently hardcodes timeout=120 with no basis, and every latency, deadline and worker-pool number in the stack will be silently invalidated the day the RTX 3090 arrives. A hardware-fingerprinted profile makes that invalidation mechanical - a failed startup assertion and a re-benchmark prompt - instead of depending on someone remembering. It is the natural companion to item 1 (the journal writes the profile's measured block) and its 'adopt now' slice is tiny.

**Sketch:** config/capacity_profile.yaml split into a 'measured' block (only ever overwritten by the api/bench/ harness and the item-1 weekly fit) and a hand-owned 'policy' block (worker pool size, sync budget, queue cap) so a re-benchmark cannot wipe ops decisions. Fingerprint: CPU model + core count, GPU present/name, RAM, Ollama/vLLM version, model digests. Startup in main.py compares the fingerprint to the running host; on mismatch it drops to a concrete conservative static profile - serial execution, worker pool 1, hard queue-length cap, reject-over-capacity with a typed reason (NOT 'no deadline', which is fail-open) - and logs 'ré-étalonnage requis'. Now: replace timeout=120 with a per-model value read from the profile plus the startup check; thread the remaining reads when the R2.6/R1.3 lanes are built. A one-off soak run (size S) writes a static derate factor and a safe max-sustained-concurrency into the measured block. ~2 days now.

## Contradictions (and resolutions)

- Capacity P1 says a fingerprint mismatch should fall back to 'no-deadline best-effort mode'. That is fail-OPEN: with no deadline and no admission bound the queue grows without limit on a 3 tok/s box and you are back to universal 120s timeouts or OOM. Resolution: the mismatch fallback is a concrete conservative static profile (serial execution, worker pool 1, hard queue-length cap, reject-over-capacity with a typed R1.3 degradation reason). Adopted in item 6.
- models.yaml P1 wants the CLAUDE.md cascade table auto-regenerated from a dev-editable YAML; rounds 1-2 repeatedly state the cascade is frozen intent that Jazzy signs and must not be unilaterally rewritten. Resolution: the manifest emits a divergence report only; reconciling it into CLAUDE.md stays a manual governance edit. Never auto-write.
- models.yaml P1 fail-closed-on-boot vs the round-2 finding that a 2-3 person team does not watch Grafana and a stack that refuses to boot at 2am is itself a hazard (and its override flag would become permanent). Resolution: fail-closed only for the two critical weights (GLiNER PII model, T4 legal model); T1-T3 mismatch logs loudly, bumps a counter, fires the T5 webhook and still serves behind a banner; override flag requires a dated expiry. Adopted in item 2.
- Model-swap P2 says replace T3/T4 weights with official AWQ releases 'before go-live'; AWQ is a GPU-only format and vLLM cannot serve it on the CPU-only box, and at ~1 tok/s a 14B/22B model on CPU is unusable. Resolution: do only the surgical removal of the community weights now; gate the actual vendor-weight swap on the 3090 arriving, with final model IDs chosen by the item-2 admission harness and the legal tier pinned to the Mistral-Small-24B class (never Ministral, which is 3B/8B).
- Capacity P2 proposes a precise per-query wall-clock ETA, a per-model-family tokenizer in the router hot path, and benchmarking at concurrency 1 and 2. On the CPU box concurrency 2 just thrashes and an 8s sync budget means every real answer goes async anyway, so the precise ETA UX is not worth building; post-3090, capping request concurrency at 2 throws away vLLM's continuous-batching throughput and conflates 'never multi-GPU' (hardware) with request concurrency (unrelated). Resolution: CPU era gets a coarse 'réponse différée, ~N min' only, measured at concurrency 1; the precise linear model and per-query ETA are deferred to post-3090, where the harness sweeps vLLM throughput vs batch size; no tokenizer in the hot path (chars/4 plus a measured per-model correction factor).
- Acceptable-use P1 casually builds a per-agent query log 'for accountability' with no access control, while P3 wraps a mere risk tag in an elaborate two-person dual-control unseal and PWA P1 / P4 each create their own identity-linked stores. The access-control posture is inconsistent across the design. Resolution: one policy for ALL identity-linked query data (corbeille, transfer journal, any abuse dataset) - named access only, logged reason, retention cap, registre des traitements entry, and CSE/CST consultation before go-live; do not build a standalone per-agent query log beyond what the corbeille already necessitates.
- Acceptable-use P3's own assessment concludes that building a per-employee log of automated misconduct-adjacent risk tags is the risk, and the policy lock protecting it is thin. Resolution: ship only the user-facing charte-reminder banner driven by a local French lexicon; NO per-identity logged tag; keep only an anonymous weekly aggregate counter per risk category. Per-identity tagging is reconsidered only after CSE sign-off AND an eval harness demonstrating an acceptable false-positive rate on a real legal/HR corpus.
- Acceptable-use P2's 'near-duplicate burst' and 'templated fan-out' abuse signals collide head-on with the canonical-answer and templated-workflow direction the project is pursuing in R2.2. Resolution: drop those two signals; keep only the structured-citizen-identifier-count tripwire (throttle-and-review, never auto-block, named owner, tuned so a benefits/tax clerk clearing 200 dossiers is not flagged).
- TCO P2's scale-to-zero batch worker depends on the async deferred lane from R2.6, which does not exist - main.py is synchronous request/response with no queue or job store. Resolution: the batch-worker design is a target for when that lane lands; the now-actions are (a) confirm the interim VM's hosting is sovereignty-compliant for government personal data - blocking - and (b) put a hard monthly euro ceiling plus a dated sunset criterion tied to the 3090 procurement into DECISIONS_NEEDED, reviewed monthly.
- Production-loop P4 wants the production feedback loop and the offline eval harness merged into one artifact; when the weekly review lapses (the first thing dropped in an incident week) both degrade together and the stable offline baseline is lost, and appending pseudonymised real user text to api/eval/ commits it to git history permanently and un-erasably. Resolution: the field corpus augments but does not merge into the hand-built offline set; field-failure entries are reviewer-paraphrased into synthetic wording (or hard PII-scrubbed) before any git commit; raw pseudonymised items live only in the TTL store; two rotating reviewers, not one.
- PWA P2's zero-JS baseline implies a synchronous POST that will hang past proxy, browser and satellite timeouts on a multi-minute answer, and the agent then resubmits (duplicates). Resolution: PWA P2 only works coupled to PWA P1 - the no-JS submit does a 303 redirect straight to the corbeille ticket view. Adopt them as one item (item 4).

## Threads to go deeper (round 4)

- acceptable-use-safety-incident: What is the concrete pre-go-live French labour-law compliance sequence for any identity-linked query data (the corbeille, the transfer journal, the abuse tripwire dataset) - specifically, is prior CSE/CST consultation plus formal information des agents required before the authenticated PWA can launch with per-agent logging, who owns that process, and what is the lead time? Round 2 assumed this was follow-up paperwork; if it is blocking it reshapes the launch plan.
- model-supply-chain-vetting: Exactly which official vendor weights become T3 and T4 once the RTX 3090 is installed (Qwen2.5-Coder-14B-AWQ? Mistral-Small-3.x-24B-AWQ?), what is the measured VRAM split against the 22.6 / 24 GB budget with Ollama T1+T2 co-resident, and given the R1.4 'auto-cascade capped at T4' rule, does T4 have to be strong enough that T5 is never load-bearing for correctness?
- pwa-client-and-notification: For the corbeille, what is the retention model for tickets whose query or answer contains Article 9 or third-party personal data - a single short TTL for everything, or content-class-dependent - and how does the droit a l'effacement operate against a store that is simultaneously the source of the feedback and eval signal? This is the load-bearing RGPD question the ticket-store design opens.
- measured-capacity-harness: Once the request journal has 6-8 weeks of production data, what specific p95 and peak-to-mean thresholds convert 'heures ouvrees, best effort, no number' into a published SLA, and what is the documented escalation if the deferred backlog's drain rate is structurally below the arrival rate before the 3090 lands (widen the window, buy the GPU sooner, shed load)?
- production-answer-quality-loop: Given that the canonical answer base, the R1.6 embedding classifier and the feedback button are all still unbuilt, what is the minimum viable version of the weekly 'revue qualite' that can run on day one using only the corbeille tickets and a thumbs-down - and what is the exact rule for turning a confirmed field failure into an eval entry without committing real or pseudonymised user text to git?

## Facets / concerns missed

- Backup, disaster recovery and single-box failure. The entire stack - inference, corbeille, Postgres state, ledger, transfer journal - runs on one on-prem box with no facet addressing RPO/RTO, a cold spare or restore procedure, or how a multi-island service for 5500 agents degrades when that box is down for a day. The ticket store makes this worse: losing it loses in-flight work with no client-side copy on shared workstations.
- Secret and key management. .env holds real ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, LITELLM_MASTER_KEY, database credentials, the future oauth2-proxy client secret and the new HMAC key for the transfer journal, on a host that currently publishes services with no auth. No rotation story, no vault, no blast-radius analysis. A single .env leak is simultaneously uncapped cloud spend and a PII-reidentification key.
- Change management and release process for the stack itself. Docker Compose, 2-3 people, no staging environment mentioned. The eval harness gates content quality but there is no deployment pipeline, no canary against a subset of the 3500 workstations, no defined rollback for a bad prompt-pack or model swap, and no way to test a compose change before it hits production.
- Adoption, onboarding and expectation-setting for 5500 agents. Nothing covers teaching agents that this is not ChatGPT, that answers take minutes, that citizen files must not be pasted in bulk, and where they report a bad answer or a system problem. A technically sound system that agents distrust, misuse or route around fails regardless of its architecture.
- Institutional liability and human-in-the-loop for high-stakes answers. The AUP facet covers misuse by agents; nothing covers the system confidently giving a wrong RGPD, statutory or benefits answer that an agent then relays to a citizen, nor an agent asking a genuinely distressing personal question. Disclaimer prompts are mentioned but not who is accountable when a 'legal tier' answer is acted on, nor any escalation-to-human path.
- Equity of a French-only assistant for a workforce whose working French varies (francophone / reo Tahiti / reo Pa'umotu). PWA P2 scopes the UI chrome and refuses Tahitian model output, which is the right call given the models, but the deeper service-equity question - whether agents who work more comfortably in Tahitian are disadvantaged by the tool - is unaddressed.

## Open questions for the operator

- Chapter V transfer basis for T5: does the marche public with the EU-Claude provider carry signed SCCs plus a documented Transfer Impact Assessment (or DPF certification), reviewed and signed off by the DSI DPO, BEFORE any production T5 call? Until the answer is yes, T5 stays disabled via the R2.5 fail-closed gate - the egress substrate in item 3 is forensics, not a lawful basis.
- Does French labour law require prior CSE/CST consultation and formal information des agents before launching the authenticated PWA with per-agent query logging, and if so who runs that process and on what timeline? This may gate the whole identity-bound design.
- Is the interim CPU worker-VM hosted on infrastructure that is contractually and legally acceptable for processing government-agent personal data (SecNumCloud / EU sovereignty posture)? If not, does inference on real agent queries have to stay on the on-prem box - accepting ~3 tok/s - until the 3090 arrives?
- Approve the written amendment to the frozen cascade: neural-chat and dolphin-mixtral are removed now; T3 and T4 become named official vendor weights when the 3090 lands. This changes the CLAUDE.md cascade table and needs Jazzy's signature.
- Who is the single named holder of the consolidated AI TCO line, and what are the concrete euro caps (T5 per day, T5 per month, total stack per month) plus the monthly kWh assumption for the electricity line?
- Is a reo Tahiti UI-chrome locale (not model output) a committed v1-plus deliverable with a named translation owner and a Fare Vana'a review cadence, or explicitly out of scope? Build the string-pack seam either way; the commitment is Jazzy's.
- Named-people capacity check on a 3-person team: confirm or descope the 2 rotating quality reviewers (~0.2 FTE standing), the canonical-answer editorial owner (~0.3 FTE), and the 2 people authorised for dual-control unseal. These are permanent commitments, not project tasks.
- SLA posture: is the operator prepared to publish 'heures ouvrees, best effort, no number' to 5500 agents until 6-8 weeks of request-journal data exist, and to defend that position to leadership rather than commit to a guessed figure now?
- Access-control and retention policy for identity-linked query data: who may read the corbeille, the transfer journal and any abuse dataset, under what logged justification, for how long - and who can compel disclosure (a manager, a court order, a future administration)?
